## Correction (added after Shep's response)

**The overshoot in this review was largely my error, not his training.**

Shep ran both quality sessions exactly as prescribed:

- **Wed 5 Aug:** 3km warm-up, 5x1km with jog recoveries, 3km cool-down
- **Sat 8 Aug:** 3km warm-up, 4x10min at threshold with jog recoveries, 3km cool-down
- **Sun 9 Aug:** 18km by feel, "knew it was down around 4:30"

The plan's target distances counted only the reps. 5x1km with 90s recoveries
inside a 3km warm-up and cool-down is **12km minimum** — the plan said 8. Four
by ten minutes at threshold is **18km** — the plan said 12. Roughly 10km of the
"12.7km overshoot" was arithmetic, and Saturday was logged as "intervals"
because 4x10min with jog recoveries reasonably reads as intervals.

So the compliance criticism is withdrawn. What stands is the physiology: 73.7km
was still run, HRV baseline still fell 43.9 → 36.3, RHR still rose 46.7 → 48.7,
ACWR still reached 1.4, across four high-heat days. The body doesn't care whose
arithmetic was wrong. Sunday at 4:29/km is a smaller point — 7 sec/km quicker
than the band, by feel, in 28.4°C; running by feel is right, but on the 20km+
runs let it settle at 4:40 or slower so the long run stays genuinely aerobic.

**The recommendation therefore changes from hold to adjust** — not to add
anything, but because the plan's numbers need to become honest. Every remaining
session now states the whole session including warm-up, recoveries and
cool-down, with a distance that matches. Easy days are trimmed so the true
weekly totals land at 66, 68, 70 (peak) and 64km — a real reduction on the
73.7km just run, which is the appropriate answer to the recovery trend.
